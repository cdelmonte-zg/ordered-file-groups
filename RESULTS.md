## Datasets

Partial overlap: the same 600,000 rows in every dataset, 12 files, each sorted by
`(col_1, col_2)`. Depth d: file i overlaps files i+1 .. i+d-1 and is disjoint from
file i+d, so the minimum number of ordered groups is d. The groups produced can be
more: the algorithm starts from `target_partitions` empty groups, so depth 1
produces 2 groups although 1 would be enough. Overlap is built on `col_1`, because
the bounds of a file are assembled from per-column min/max.

The other four reproduce the cases of 2026-09-28: 3, 12, 15 and 1000 files in which
every file overlaps every other.

## Round 3: observations

1. Control: where both binaries produce 2 groups (depth 1 and 2), plans and times
   are the same.
2. The benefit depends on three things together: the ordering that is kept, the
   number of concurrent ordered streams, and the memory budget. At 128 MB, 3 and 4
   groups remove `SortExec` and the spills of the final aggregate. At 6 and 12
   groups the final aggregate spills again and most of the gain is lost. At 256 MB
   and 512 MB the same plans do not spill in the final aggregate.
3. Spilling, by operator, as the metrics show it:
   - Sort: no modified plan has a `SortExec`, so there are no sort spills.
   - Final aggregate: no spills in the modified plan with 3 and 4 groups at every
     pool size, and with 6, 12 and 1000 groups at 256 MB and 512 MB. At 128 MB it
     spills with 6, 12, 15 and 1000 groups, in some cases more often than in the
     original plan (12 fully overlapping files: 40 against 24; 1000 files: 82
     against 24). With 15 groups it spills at 128 MB and 256 MB.
   - `RepartitionExec` with `preserve_order=true`: spills in most modified plans,
     more with more groups and less with a larger pool. With 15 groups the count
     stays at about 150 at every pool size.
4. 1000 files: slower than the original at 128 MB, faster at 256 MB and 512 MB.
   Process RSS of the modified plan is about 1.7 GB at every pool size, against 0.5
   to 0.6 GB. Increasing the pool did not materially change the high process RSS.
   Which allocations make it up was not measured.
5. 15 files, target 10: the original fails at 128 MB and 256 MB in all runs and
   completes at 512 MB, where it is faster than the modified plan. With the modified
   plan an intermittent allocation failure was observed at 128 MB
   (`SortPreservingMergeExec`, which cannot spill).
6. `ulimit -n 1024`, 1000 files, modified plan: `IO error: Too many open files (os error 24) at path "/tmp/.tmpUATFfp/.tmpsxHSMn"`. This identifies the open
   that failed, a temporary file, not which files held the other descriptors. The
   CLI exits with status 0.
7. Not explained: depth 1 is slower than depth 2, with the same plan shape in both
   binaries.

## What this supports

Accepting extra groups can help under a limited pool, it introduces costs of its
own, and it does not dominate the original plan. This is evidence for discussing
how to choose the number of groups, not for removing the check unconditionally.
